My research develops and applies theoretical methods for describing nuclear quantum effects, such as zero-point energy, anharmonicity, nuclear delocalization, and tunneling, in molecular systems and chemical reactions involving hydrogen nuclei. This work is carried out in the [Hammes-Schiffer group](https://chemistry.princeton.edu/faculty-research/faculty/sharon-hammes-schiffer) as part of the DOE Center for Hybrid Approaches in Solar Energy to Liquid Fuels (CHASE).

The nuclear-electronic orbital (NEO) approach treats specified nuclei, typically protons, quantum mechanically on the same level as the electrons, thereby incorporating nuclear quantum effects directly into electronic structure calculations. My work follows two directions within this framework.

### NEO method development

I develop accurate and efficient NEO methods for capturing nuclear quantum effects in molecular systems. Representative targets include proton vibrational excitation energies and tunneling splittings, which are sensitive probes of the coupling between electronic and protonic motion. A central goal is to achieve this accuracy at a computational cost that allows applications to larger and more complex systems.

### Applications to chemical reactions

I develop NEO-based protocols for predicting the thermodynamics and kinetics of chemical reactions involving proton and hydrogen transfer.

- **Thermodynamics.** I developed a general protocol for relative pKa prediction that efficiently incorporates anharmonic effects into Gibbs free energies within the NEO framework ([J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.5c11332)).
- **Kinetics.** NEO general rate theory (NEO-GRT) captures hydrogen tunneling effects, including deep tunneling, and enables accurate and efficient calculation of rate constants and H/D kinetic isotope effects from full-dimensional molecular calculations ([J. Chem. Theory Comput. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jctc.6c01175)).
- **Collaborations with experiment.** I apply these tools to proton-coupled redox chemistry in collaboration with experimental groups, for example in intramolecular hydrogen-bonded networks that tune anthraquinone redox properties ([J. Am. Chem. Soc. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1021/jacs.6c15431)).

### Software

- **[NEO-GRT package](https://github.com/shsgroup/NEO-GRT)**: package for NEO general rate theory calculations of rate constants and KIEs (maintainer).
- **[Q-Chem](https://www.q-chem.com/)**: contributor to the development of NEO methods in the Q-Chem quantum chemistry program.
