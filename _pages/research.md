My research develops and applies theoretical methods for describing nuclear quantum effects, such as zero-point energy, nuclear delocalization, and tunneling, in molecular systems and chemical reactions involving hydrogen nuclei. This work is carried out in the [Hammes-Schiffer group](https://chemistry.princeton.edu/faculty-research/faculty/sharon-hammes-schiffer) as part of the DOE Center for Hybrid Approaches in Solar Energy to Liquid Fuels (CHASE).

The nuclear-electronic orbital (NEO) approach treats specified nuclei, typically protons, quantum mechanically on the same level as the electrons, thereby incorporating nuclear quantum effects directly into electronic structure calculations. My work follows two directions within this framework.

### NEO method development

I develop accurate and efficient NEO methods for computing proton vibrational excitation energies and tunneling splittings. A central goal is to capture the coupling between electronic and protonic motion, which governs these quantities, at a computational cost that allows applications to larger molecular systems.

### NEO general rate theory for chemical reactions

I apply the NEO framework to chemical reactions through NEO general rate theory (NEO-GRT). This approach captures hydrogen tunneling effects and enables accurate and efficient calculation of rate constants and kinetic isotope effects (KIEs) for hydrogen transfer reactions, which can be compared directly with experimental measurements.

### Software

- **NEO-GRT package**: Python package for NEO general rate theory calculations of rate constants and KIEs (maintainer).
- **[Q-Chem](https://www.q-chem.com/)**: contributor to the development of NEO methods in the Q-Chem quantum chemistry program.
