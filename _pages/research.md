My research develops and applies theoretical methods for describing nuclear quantum effects, such as zero-point energy, nuclear delocalization, and tunneling, in chemical reactions involving the transfer of hydrogen nuclei. This work is carried out in the [Hammes-Schiffer group](https://chemistry.princeton.edu/faculty-research/faculty/sharon-hammes-schiffer) as part of the DOE Center for Hybrid Approaches in Solar Energy to Liquid Fuels (CHASE).

### Nuclear-electronic orbital (NEO) methods

The NEO approach treats specified nuclei, typically protons, quantum mechanically on the same level as the electrons, thereby incorporating nuclear quantum effects directly into electronic structure calculations. I am interested in improving the accuracy and efficiency of NEO methods, with particular emphasis on the treatment of electron-proton correlation.

### Electron-proton correlation functionals

The accuracy of NEO density functional theory depends critically on the electron-proton correlation (epc) functional. I work on the development and parameterization of epc functionals, using high-level NEO wavefunction methods as reference data, to obtain reliable protonic densities and energies at the cost of density functional theory.

### Nonadiabatic rate theory for hydrogen transfer

Many hydrogen transfer and proton-coupled electron transfer reactions are vibronically nonadiabatic. I develop rate theories formulated within the NEO framework (NEO-NATST and NEO-GRT) to compute rate constants and kinetic isotope effects (KIEs), and apply them to hydrogen transfer reactions in collaboration with experimental groups.
