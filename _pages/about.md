I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Chemistry at Princeton University, working with [Prof. Sharon Hammes-Schiffer](https://chemistry.princeton.edu/faculty-research/faculty/sharon-hammes-schiffer). My research focuses on multicomponent quantum chemistry within the nuclear-electronic orbital (NEO) framework, in which specified nuclei, typically protons, are treated quantum mechanically on the same footing as the electrons.

My work follows two directions. The first is the development of accurate and efficient NEO methods for capturing nuclear quantum effects, with proton vibrational excitation energies and tunneling splittings as representative targets. The second is the application of the NEO framework to chemical reactions. I developed a protocol for pKa prediction that efficiently incorporates anharmonicity into Gibbs free energies, and NEO general rate theory (NEO-GRT) captures hydrogen tunneling effects to enable accurate and efficient calculation of rate constants and kinetic isotope effects. I maintain the NEO-GRT package and contribute to the development of the [Q-Chem](https://www.q-chem.com/) quantum chemistry program.

Before coming to Princeton, I received my B.S. in Chemistry with a minor in Computer Science from KAIST, where I worked with Prof. Young Min Rhee on chemical-protein interactions using classical molecular dynamics simulations and docking, and subsequently on the computational photodynamics of OLED materials.
